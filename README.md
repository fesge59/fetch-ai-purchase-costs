## What you're actually buying when you buy Fetch AI

The token merger worked by folding two other tokens into FET:

- 1 AGIX (SingularityNET) converts to 0.433350 FET
- 1 OCEAN (Ocean Protocol) converts to 0.433226 FET

Ocean Protocol then walked out of the alliance on 8–9 October 2025, without the prior notice Fetch.ai says it expected. What followed was messy. Fetch.ai and three token holders filed a class action in the Southern District of New York on 4 November 2025 (case 1:25-cv-9210), alleging that roughly 661 million OCEAN tokens had been converted into about 286.46 million FET and that most of that FET was then sold into the market. Blockchain analytics firm Bubblemaps traced around 270 million FET moving to Binance and to the OTC desk GSR, and noted it couldn't confirm who ultimately sold, though it said transfers of that shape usually point to liquidation.

The fight ended with a handshake rather than a verdict. Under a settlement mediated by the validator GeoStaking, Ocean Protocol returns roughly 286 million FET (around $120 million at the time the offer was made) to Fetch.ai's community pool, and Fetch.ai drops its legal claims. Ocean's side has consistently denied wrongdoing and argued that market conditions, not its token movements, drove FET's price down.

Why this belongs in a buying guide: a chunk of supply that the market once assumed was gone is being returned to a community pool, and the alliance is now effectively Fetch.ai, SingularityNET and Cudos rather than the three-way merger it advertised. ## The three places FET gets bought

Fetch.ai's token is listed on centralised exchanges and available through wallets and on-chain venues, and those routes differ in ways that matter more than the branding suggests.

**Centralised exchanges (CEX)** are the shortest path for a first purchase. You register, complete identity verification, fund the account by card or bank transfer, then place an order. You get an order book with visible depth, a published fee schedule, 2FA options and cold storage on the platform side. You are trusting a custodian with the assets, which is the trade-off.

**Non-custodial wallets** keep the keys with you. Some support swapping tokens directly in-app, and some allow card purchases without routing through an exchange first. You're responsible for your seed phrase, and you should verify the contract address before confirming anything.

**Decentralised exchanges (DEX)** execute swaps through smart contracts with no registration and no KYC. You connect a compatible wallet, pick the pair, set slippage tolerance and confirm. You pay gas, and liquidity on smaller pairs can be thin enough that the price you get is worse than what a centralised book shows.

## Market order, limit order, or Convert?

A market order fills immediately at whatever the book offers, which is what you want if the price matters less than getting in. A limit order waits at your price, but at VIP 0 through VIP 3 it saves you nothing on fees, so the only benefit is price control. Convert is the shortcut when you already hold USDT or ETH and the amount is small enough that a spread of a few basis points is cheaper than the friction of a spot order.

For a first FET purchase of a few hundred dollars, Convert or a market order is fine. For anything larger, a limit order placed near the current price usually gets filled without chasing the book, and you avoid paying up during a spike.
